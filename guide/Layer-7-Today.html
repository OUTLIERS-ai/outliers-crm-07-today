Layer 7 · Today

Attention is the thing you run out of

Not contacts, not information, not tools. The limit is how many people you can genuinely think about in a day, and it is smaller than anyone admits.

What the last layer left you with

Your system is safe and orderly and full of useful information. It works through its queue in the order the queue was built, which means something that happened this morning sits behind whatever was already in there.

What this layer builds

One page, each morning. A short list of people, in the order they actually deserve your attention, with the reason on every line.

Layer 7 · How it works

How it works

Ordered by how quickly something goes stale, not by how important the person is.

Some things stop being worth doing very quickly

A reply is worth answering now. The same reply answered in three days is a different, worse thing, and left much longer it is slightly embarrassing. A contact who changed jobs is worth a note straight away and nothing much a month later.

Other things do not decay at all. Someone you have been meaning to catch up with for a while will still be there tomorrow.

So your list is ordered by perishability. The person at the top is not more important than the person below them. Their reason for being there is simply going off faster.

Every line says why

A list without reasons is a list you argue with, and then ignore. When each line says replied this morning or thirty days since you spoke, you either act or you consciously decide not to, and both are fine. What you do not do is stare at it.

Some people never appear

Anyone on hold. Anyone marked personal, so friends and family are never in a list of people to work through. Anyone you have already decided is not a fit. The list is what to act on, and the fastest way to make someone stop reading it is to fill it with things they have already said no to.

Everything else steps aside on its own

Anything that does not earn a place today does not pile up waiting for you. It parks itself and comes back if something changes. That is what stops the list becoming another source of guilt, which is what happens to every to-do system eventually.

How long it should be

The installer asks how many people you can genuinely speak to in a day, and that is the length. If the list is longer than you can actually do, it stops being a plan and becomes a reminder that you are behind.

Layer 7 · Finishing

Now use it

The one that changes how a working day feels.

Tomorrow morning, open your list before you open your inbox. Work only from it until it is done.

Two things usually happen. You get through it faster than you expected, because deciding who to deal with is most of the work and that decision was already made. And you notice how much of your usual morning was spent reacting to whoever shouted loudest rather than to whatever was actually most perishable.

Do it once before you judge it.

If you already had something like this

Your test: how do you currently decide who to contact first? If the honest answer is whoever is most recent in your inbox, that is your inbox setting your priorities, and your inbox does not know which of those things goes stale fastest.

What this layer leaves unsolved

You are fast now, and working on assumptions. The order rests on a belief about which signals matter, and that belief has never been tested against what actually happens. There is also nothing telling you whether the system is still working, or quietly doing nothing at all.
